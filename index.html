Congress

Cloture on the motion to proceed takes 60 votes. Backers are aiming for about 70 to pressure the House (Politico, Oct. 29), and are courting more than a dozen Senate Democrats (Inside EPA). Most members have said nothing in public since introduction, so "No statement found" is the default, not a position.

After the midterms: Democrats won control of the Senate on Nov. 3, and the House was still uncalled overnight. The new Senate is not seated until after the Nov. 9 vote, so the vote count below is the current Senate. One supporter, Sen. Husted (R-OH), lost his seat to Sherrod Brown.

      Where they agree, and where they fight

      Industry and the big green groups agree on more than either side lets on. Here's what they share, the fixes both say are easy, and where the fight actually is.

      Common ground: what industry and the big green groups both like

      Industry means the clean energy trades, API and oil and gas groups, business groups, building trades unions, and supportive utilities. Green groups means NRDC, the Sierra Club, and Earthjustice: the three heaviest hitters seeking changes, none of which has endorsed the bill.

      Former administration officials

      Former officials, by name

      Most of the support comes from one letter organized by the Institute for Progress. It was signed by 19 former cabinet members and White House principals from seven administrations, from George H.W. Bush through Trump's second term. The critics are mostly Biden-era environmental lawyers.
